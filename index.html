E-Commerce Customer Segmentation — RFM

20% of customers generate 64% of revenue. Retention budget moved from blanket discounting to 866 named accounts.

  • Cleaned 541,909 raw transactions down to 392,692 valid rows in Pandas — duplicates, null customer IDs, cancellations and non-positive lines, each exclusion logged and counted.
  • Scored all 4,338 customers in PostgreSQL with CTEs and NTILE(5) window functions, concatenating a three-digit RFM score and mapping it to 11 business segments.
  • Surfaced 646 lapsing high-value accounts holding £1.03M of historic spend — the highest-ROI win-back list in the base.

02 Jun 2026

SaaS Subscription Funnel & Churn

62% of cancellations happen in the first three months. That makes onboarding the lever, not win-back.

  • Instrumented a four-stage funnel from 66,000 raw event logs, collapsing repeat emits to first touch and voiding out-of-sequence stages that would otherwise inflate every conversion rate.
  • Isolated the single worst leak — 57% of users lost between First Use and Paid Upgrade, at a median of 12 days.
  • Built cohort retention and MRR movement in PostgreSQL using generate_series to expand each customer into active months; monthly MRR churn of 4.2% means ~£196K a year must be replaced before the business grows.
  • Pandas
  • Matplotlib
  • PostgreSQL
  • Cohort analysis
  • DAX

03 Mar 2026

Retail Inventory & Margin Optimization

234 SKUs carrying £1,786 a day of gross margin were about to run dry. 174 of those shortages needed a transfer, not a purchase order.

  • Standardised a 6,220-row warehouse extract carrying four SKU spellings, #N/A costs, negative counts and duplicate rows, then merged it with the product master to derive margin per SKU.
  • Joined stock, 120 days of sales and the warehouse dimension in PostgreSQL to compute daily sales velocity, days of supply and ABC class across 1,200 SKUs in 5 warehouses.
  • Ranked the buying list by margin at risk per day rather than by low stock — so a penny line and the best seller are never treated as the same emergency.

About

Analyst first, dashboard second

I'm a Business Analyst and Power BI developer based in Bengaluru. Most of my work starts the same way: someone has a report that takes hours to produce and still doesn't answer the question. I rebuild the path from raw data to the decision — cleaning and validating the inputs, modelling them in SQL, then publishing something stakeholders can actually use.

What I care about is the part people skip. Whether a null means zero or unknown. Whether a rate is measured against the right denominator. Whether the number on the card would survive someone checking it. The case studies in my projects document those decisions deliberately, because that's the difference between a chart and an analysis.

I hold a PGDM in Big Data Analytics and a BBA (Hons.) in Marketing — which means I'm as comfortable in a stakeholder conversation as in a query editor.
